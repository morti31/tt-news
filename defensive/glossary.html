Glossary

The terms used for defensive table tennis, and how The Chop works.

Terms

Chop
A backspin stroke played from well behind the table, usually against topspin. The defender's main shot.
Chopper
A player whose game is built on chopping. The Chop splits defenders into three styles; see below.
Push
A short backspin stroke played over the table, usually against backspin.
Float
A chop with little or no spin, disguised to look heavy so the attacker misjudges it.
Inverted rubber
Smooth rubber with the pimples facing in. Gives the most spin; almost every attacker uses it on both sides.
Long pimples
Rubber with long, thin pimples facing out. Returns much of the opponent's spin, so a topspin can come back as backspin. Many choppers use it on the backhand.
Short pimples
Rubber with short pimples facing out. Less spin, more speed and a flatter ball; some defenders use it to block and hit.
Anti-spin
Slick rubber that takes most of the spin off the ball. Rare at the top level.
Twiddling
Turning the bat mid-rally so the forehand and backhand rubbers swap, to change the spin the opponent receives.
Counter-attack
A defender switching from chopping to topspin, often against a weak loop.
Expedite rule
If a game lasts 10 minutes and fewer than 18 points have been scored, the server must win the point within 13 returns or lose it. Applies for the rest of the match. Long defensive matches trigger it most.
World ranking
The ITTF world ranking, published weekly by World Table Tennis. Points come from a player's best results over the past year, including youth events.

The three styles

Defends from well back with heavy backspin and variation, attacking now and then.
Chops, but counter-attacks and plays topspin as a regular part of the game.
Defends closer to the table with blocks, pushes and long pimples, and disrupts rather than chops.

Defensive style

How we decide: The Chop puts every defender in one of three styles. A player is only included when a source we can open describes them as a defender; long pimples alone don't count. The type follows how the sources describe their game, and each player's page quotes the exact words. Where no source describes the game in detail, the type is our provisional call. The definitions are ours, based on:

  • Martin Spin's ranking of defensive and special styles, which describes how each player plays
  • Butterfly's coaching guide, Player archetypes: defensive styles
